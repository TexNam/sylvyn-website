Context

Why Scotland

Scotland already generates more renewable electricity than it can use or move south. What is scarce is land of the right scale, in the right place, with a real path onto that power.

More generation than demand

Scotland produced 38.5 TWh of renewable electricity in 2025. About 18.4 GW of renewables was online by mid-2026. The system is not short of wind. It is short of demand in the north, and of wires that can move the surplus south.

That is the case for locating load here. Generation already exists. A site still has to connect to it.

What the constraint payments show

When the boundary is full, the grid pays wind to turn down. Renewable Energy Foundation puts that at about £394 million in 2024 and £382 million in 2025, almost all of the volume in Scotland. NESO’s wider books show £1.7 billion of thermal constraint cost across Great Britain in 2024/25, with Scotland the main congestion driver. That £1.7 billion is not a Scotland-only bill.

These figures show surplus generation. They are not a tariff, and they are not a connection offer. A campus does not simply collect the curtailed megawatt-hours.

Figures

Scotland’s renewables, in numbers

~18.4 GW Renewables online mid-2026. About 11 GW onshore, 4.3 GW offshore, 1.7 GW hydro, under 1 GW solar.
38.5 TWh Renewable electricity generated in Scotland in 2025.
~£380–394m Recent annual wind turn-down, mostly Scottish volume. GB thermal constraints were £1.7bn in FY2024/25. Scotland was the main driver. Not a Scotland-only invoice.
~99 GW Planning pipeline mid-2026. Roughly 30 GW offshore wind, 18 GW onshore wind, 33 GW battery.
Policy ambitions Onshore 20 GW by 2030. Offshore: older 8–11 GW by 2030 under review; up to 40 GW new by 2040 (Jan 2026 update). Solar proposed 4–6 GW by 2030. Nuclear: Torness only (not renewable).

Figures sourced from Scottish Government Energy Statistics, DESNZ regional capacity, NESO, and REF as dated. Ambitions are policy goals; delivery depends on consenting, grid, and markets. The £1.7bn figure is a GB thermal-constraint total, not a Scotland-only invoice.
